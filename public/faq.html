What is Home Responsibilities Protection (HRP)?

Between 6 April 1978 and 5 April 2010, HRP protected the State Pension of people who stayed at home to look after a child under 16 or care for someone. It was meant to be added automatically for the person Child Benefit was paid to, but it was sometimes missed.

Does receiving Child Benefit mean I'm owed money?

No, not automatically. Many people already have the right HRP, and years you worked full time don't need it. We look at your Child Benefit years, your work history, your National Insurance record and your DWP pension details before deciding whether a claim is supported. If it isn't, we tell you and close the case at no cost.

Which years do you ask HMRC about?

Only the tax years in which you received Child Benefit for a child under 16 and did not work full time for the whole year. We list each year and the reason on the request you sign. We never ask for your whole record.

What about my partner or the other parent?

We ask for their name, date of birth and National Insurance number if you know them. If you don't, for example after a divorce, separation, bereavement or loss of contact, just say so; we record the reason and review it before any claim is prepared.

When do I sign the claim form (CF411)?

Only after we've seen the HMRC and DWP evidence and it supports a claim. You'll see the completed form and sign it yourself before it's sent.

Do I need to log into my HMRC account?

No. You can share your National Insurance record from your online account if you want to, which can speed things up, but it's optional.

Who pays me?

DWP pays any arrears and increase straight to you. We can never receive your pension or arrears.

How much does it cost?

30% plus VAT (36% in total) of the arrears paid to you, invoiced after the arrears are confirmed, with 14 days to pay. No fee on weekly increases, and nothing to pay if no arrears are paid. See our fees.

Can I cancel?

Yes. Within 14 days of signing you pay nothing, even if we've started. After that you can still stop at any time.

How long does it take?

HMRC and DWP currently take several months. We chase them and keep you updated.

Can I claim for a parent who has died?

Yes, if you are dealing with their estate. We'll ask for the death certificate and the grant of probate or letters of administration.

Will this affect my benefits or tax?

Arrears are taxable, and a higher State Pension can reduce means-tested benefits such as Pension Credit. Citizens Advice and Age UK can give free advice.
