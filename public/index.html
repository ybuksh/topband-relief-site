Who may be affected

Most people affected are women now in their 60s, 70s and 80s

How it works

You answer a few questions. We deal with HMRC and DWP.

  1. Check

    Answer a few simple questions. It takes about a minute. We call you to talk it through, with no obligation.

  2. Sign up

    We send your fee agreement and authority forms, filled in for you to read before you sign. Sign online or on paper. You have 14 days to cancel free.

  3. Records

    We confirm your identity electronically, then ask HMRC for your National Insurance record for the specific years that matter, and ask DWP for your pension details.

  4. Claim

    Only if the records show you're owed something do we prepare the HRP claim form (CF411). You check and sign it, then we send it to HMRC.

  5. Collect

    If your record is corrected, DWP pays any arrears straight to you and increases your weekly pension. This usually takes 6 to 12 months because of HMRC's backlog.

Questions

Common questions

Is this a scam?

No. We are an independent company registered with HMRC as a tax agent and supervised by HMRC for anti-money laundering. That registration doesn't mean HMRC endorses us. We will never ask for your bank card, PIN, passwords or any payment upfront. You'll sign a written agreement that sets out our fee before we do anything, and you can cancel within 14 days at no cost, even if we've already started work.

Does receiving Child Benefit mean I'm owed money?

Not automatically. Many people who received Child Benefit already have the right HRP on their record, and HRP only went to the person the Child Benefit was paid to. We look at your HMRC and DWP records first, and only prepare a claim if they show one is supported. If they don't, we tell you and there's nothing to pay.

Can I claim myself without paying a fee?

Yes. You can apply to HMRC directly through GOV.UK for free. We offer an optional service that handles the forms, evidence and follow-up for you.

Do I need any documents to start?

No. You can start with a few answers. We gather the evidence HMRC needs, and only ask for ID if our electronic check can't confirm it. If you already use your HMRC online account, you can share your National Insurance record with us to speed things up, but you don't have to.

What will I be asked to sign?

A fee agreement, an HMRC form 64-8 so we can deal with HMRC for you, and a DWP letter of authority. We fill them in fully before you see them, so you can read everything before signing. Sign online or ask for paper copies by post. We never change a document after you've signed it; if anything needs correcting, we send you a new version. The claim form itself (CF411) is only sent to you to sign later, once your records show a claim is supported.

Why do you ask which years I worked full time?

HMRC asks agents to request only the tax years that matter, not your whole record. Years when you worked full time already count towards your pension, so we leave them out.

Can I speak to someone?

Yes. Call us free on 0800 999 999, weekdays 9am to 5pm, or request a call back at the end of the check. Once you're a client you can also message us by WhatsApp or text.

I don't use email or the internet. Can you still help?

Yes. We can do everything by phone and post: paper copies of every document with a prepaid envelope, letters instead of emails, and payment by cheque or by card over the phone. A family member can help too, as long as you're happy for them to.

Will HMRC contact me?

Possibly. When we ask HMRC for your National Insurance record, HMRC may contact you directly to check you authorised the request. That's normal. If you're ever unsure whether a call or letter is genuine, call us or HMRC on a number you trust.

Do you receive my money, or act as my attorney?

No. DWP pays any arrears straight to you, and HMRC and DWP never pay agents. We aren't your attorney, appointee or deputy, and the authorities you sign don't let us receive or redirect any of your money.

Could a check reduce my pension?

No. Checking your record can't reduce your State Pension. The outcome is either a correction that increases it, or no change.

How can I check how my claim is going?

Use the "Check your claim" page with your reference number, your surname and the last four digits of your phone number. You can also call us free on 0800 999 999.

How long does it take?

HMRC currently has a large backlog. People are waiting between three and eleven months for a decision, and DWP pays arrears after that. We keep you updated throughout.

Will it affect my tax or benefits?

Arrears are taxed as income for the years they relate to. A higher pension can also reduce means-tested benefits such as Pension Credit or Housing Benefit. We'll explain this before you decide, and Citizens Advice or Age UK can give free benefits advice.

Can I claim for a parent who has died?

Yes. If your mother or another relative may have been affected, the person dealing with their estate can claim any arrears that were owed to them.

Is the fee charged on my weekly pension increase?

No. We only charge on the backdated arrears you actually receive. The increase to your weekly pension is yours in full.

When and how do I pay?

Only after DWP has confirmed and paid your arrears. We send an invoice with the DWP figures, and payment is due 14 days from the invoice date. You can pay through a secure online link, by bank transfer, by cheque or by card over the phone, and we can agree a payment plan if you need more time.

What if I can't pay straight away?

Call us. We'll agree a payment plan that works for you. If an invoice goes unpaid we send friendly reminders and talk to you about options first. We'd only send a final written notice after that, and we take your circumstances into account. We won't chase payment while a complaint is open.

Are you part of HMRC or DWP?

No. We are an independent company registered with HMRC as a tax agent, acting on your behalf. We are not part of, or endorsed by, HMRC, DWP or the government.
